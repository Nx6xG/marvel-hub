The Lore

From 1962 comic roots to the current state of the multiverse — everything you need to truly understand Doomsday.

Chapter I

Who is Doctor Doom?

Victor von Doom debuted in 1962 in Fantastic Four #5 and is considered the greatest villain Marvel ever created. He is monarch of the small Eastern European state of Latveria, a genius on eye level with Reed Richards — and at the same time one of the most powerful sorcerers of the Marvel universe. Science, magic and an absolute claim to power in one person: that is what makes him unique.

His core motive: Doom is convinced the world would be better under his rule — and the frightening part is that in the comics he is occasionally proven right. He is not a destroyer like Thanos but a would-be savior with a boundless ego, who wanted to free his mother from hell and blames Reed Richards for his scarred face.

Chapter II

The Kang crisis and the great pivot

This film was originally called “Avengers: The Kang Dynasty”. Kang the Conqueror (Jonathan Majors) had been built up as the saga villain since Loki season 1 — including the Council of Kangs in Quantumania. After Majors' conviction, Marvel parted ways with him in December 2023, and the saga needed a new center.

At Comic-Con in July 2024 came the historic moment: the Russos return, the film is called Doomsday — and under the mask is Robert Downey Jr. Marvel replaced the failed plan not with a stopgap but with the biggest character still left, played by the face of the MCU itself.

Chapter III

Sacred Timeline, TVA and variants

Loki season 1 established the rules: there was a Multiversal War between countless timelines. A Kang variant (“He Who Remains”) ended it by pruning everything down to a single Sacred Timeline, guarded by the TVA. Sylvie's regicide reopened the jungle of branches — the multiverse has been sprawling uncontrolled ever since.

In season 2, Loki sacrifices himself and becomes the God of Stories: at the end of time he holds all timelines together with his own hands, like a living tree. Remember that — it is the most important open Chekhov's gun of the saga.

Chapter IV

Incursions — when universes collide

Multiverse of Madness delivered the term that drives Doomsday: an incursion is the collision of two universes in which one or both are completely destroyed — triggered when the borders between worlds are violated too often.

We have already seen one: Strange visits a collapsing universe in ruins, and at the end Clea picks him up to repair an incursion “that you caused”. In the comics, incursions are the direct trigger of Secret Wars — the downfall of all worlds, from whose wreckage Doom forges his Battleworld.

“Gods? The only god this world needs wears a mask of iron.”
— in essence, every other Doom comic page since 1962
Chapter V

Secret Wars: the blueprint

Two comic templates carry the name: the original Secret Wars (1984) — and Jonathan Hickman's Secret Wars (2015), the actual model. There the multiverse dies through incursions, and at the last moment Doom seizes the power of the Beyonders.

He creates Battleworld: a patchwork planet made from the remains of destroyed universes, which he rules as God Emperor Doom — with a rewritten reality in which nobody remembers the old world. Only a handful of survivors stand against him, first among them Reed Richards. If Doomsday follows this structure even roughly, we know where the journey is headed.

Chapter VI

Franklin Richards — the most powerful child of the multiverse

The post-credit scene of First Steps is the direct entry into Doomsday: four years later, Sue finds little Franklin with a figure in a green cloak and metal mask — Doom kneels before the boy. In the comics, Franklin is an omega-level reality shaper who can create entire universes — and in Hickman's Secret Wars his power keeps Doom's Battleworld alive.

If the MCU follows that, Franklin is not simply a child in danger. He is the battery Doom wants to play god with.

Chapter VII

Anchor beings, Earth-838 and the Fox legacy

Deadpool & Wolverine explained two decisive concepts: every universe has an anchor being whose death makes the whole timeline die — and the old Fox universe exists as its own branch in the MCU multiverse, including the Void as a dump for discarded characters.

Add Earth-838 with its Illuminati and Beast's post-credit appearance in The Marvels. The walls between the Fox past and the MCU present have long been riddled with holes — Doomsday only has to tear them down. That is exactly what half the cast is there for.

Chapter VIII

What else is on the board

Sentry & Void: the MCU has a world-destroyer-level being on the New Avengers' team — unstable, traumatized, and exactly the kind of weapon that gets fought over in a multiverse war.

Mephisto: Sacha Baron Cohen has been established as Marvel's devil since Ironheart — a deal-maker who becomes a heavyweight of his own in the long run.

The Council of Kangs: never resolved. Thousands of Kang variants are theoretically still waiting — and in the comics it is Doom who cleans up what Kang never finishes.

Wiccan: Billy Maximoff lives (Agatha All Along) — Wanda's son is a reality mage, and half the Young Avengers founding roster is complete with him.

Chapter IX

Latveria — the country soon to be on every map

In the comics, Latveria is a small Eastern European state between Romania, Serbia and Hungary: picturesque villages, Doombots as police, zero crime, zero freedom. The trick that separates Doom from every other villain: he is a head of state with diplomatic immunity. You cannot simply arrest him — under international law, any attack on Doom is an attack on his country.

For Doomsday that means: the Avengers get an opponent against whom fist justice triggers diplomatic crises. And his people? Mostly genuinely love him — Latveria has no poverty, no wars, no elections. The most uncomfortable question the comics have asked for 60 years: what if the tyrant delivers?

Chapter X

The road map to the finale

This is the track ahead of us: Doomsday (December 2026) opens the two-parter — by everything structure and comics suggest, with an ending that hurts. In between lies a year Marvel will presumably fill with targeted series, before Secret Wars (December 2027) ends the saga and reshuffles the deck: a unified timeline, mutants included, an X-Men reboot behind it.

The mnemonic for everything released until then: if it is about variants, incursions, anchor beings or Franklin, it is required material. The rest is optional. This dossier keeps sorting that — and is updated after every trailer.

„New mask, same task."
— Robert Downey Jr., San Diego Comic-Con, Juli 2024

The Theory Table

The hottest fan theories with an editorial plausibility rating — based on comics, cast list and leaks.

After Secret Wars: a new, unified timeline with mutants

The saga's meta purpose: Secret Wars as an elegant reset that merges the Fox legacy, Sony contracts and 17 years of continuity into a fresh timeline — with mutants who were "always there". After that: an X-Men reboot. Practically every insider source points this way.

90 %

Doomsday ends with the birth of Battleworld

Comic template

The obvious two-parter structure after Hickman: the heroes lose, the incursions win — and the cliffhanger is Doom's stitched-together world, on which Secret Wars then takes place. Infinity War proved the Russos have the courage for a villain victory.

88 %

Franklin Richards is Doom's power source

Comic template

In the comics, Franklin's universe-creating power feeds Battleworld. The MCU has paved the way: Doom stands next to the boy in the F4 post-credit scene. The film's emotional engine could simply be: Reed and Sue want their child back — from a god.

82 %

Loki's timeline tree is the real target

Loki physically holds all timelines together. Whoever wants to control the multiverse or let it collapse must go through him. Either Doom breaks the tree — or Loki has to make the hardest decision of the saga: let branches die to save the trunk. Otherwise Hiddleston's casting makes little sense.

78 %

Hugh Jackman shows up (again)

Not officially in the cast — but his Wolverine survived Deadpool & Wolverine, his universe hangs on him as its anchor being, and Secret Wars without the best X-Man would be a strange farewell party. If not in Doomsday, then in the finale.

72 %

The Council of Kangs gets wiped out by Doom

Comic logic

Elegant for Marvel: the open Kang thread is used for characterization — Doom eliminates the Council in one sequence and proves what league he plays in. The SDCC footage (Doom stops Stormbreaker with one hand) hints at exactly this power demonstration.

68 %

Chris Evans is NOT playing Steve Rogers

Evans is confirmed, his role is not. He waved off Steve comebacks for years — but gleefully played Johnny Storm in 2024. A Human Torch variant or a dark Steve from another universe would be the more interesting road; old-man Steve as an emotional coda remains conceivable.

60 %

The dead return as variants

The multiverse makes every farewell negotiable: an Iron Man variant, a Black Widow, a T'Challa from another world. Marvel will have to use this sparingly or it devalues Endgame — but one or two targeted appearances are considered certain. The new Black Panther could be exactly such a variant.

58 %

Doom is a Tony Stark variant

Hotly debated

Why else RDJ? The theory: in another universe, Tony didn't become Iron Man but Doom — a Tony who could never stop "saving the world". Against it: the Russos stress that Doom is a completely separate character. For it: Marvel doesn't cast RDJ without a reason.

55 %

Mephisto is the puppet master behind Doom

Since Ironheart, Mephisto is real. The theory: Doom's magic has a price, and the devil collects in the end. Currently more wishful thinking than evidence — in the comics, Doom never needed to play second fiddle to anyone.

30 %

The Nerd Glossary

Incursion MoM · 2022
Collision of two universes that erases one or both. The engine of Secret Wars.
Anchor being Deadpool & Wolverine
A person on whose existence the stability of their entire universe hangs. If they die, the timeline dies.
Sacred Timeline Loki S1
The one permitted timeline that He Who Remains left standing — until Sylvie killed him and everything branched again.
TVA Time Variance Authority
Agency outside space and time. Formerly timeline police, today multiverse disaster control.
Battleworld Comics
Doom's patchwork planet made of fragments of destroyed universes. Presumed setting of Secret Wars.
God Emperor Doom Secret Wars 2015
Doom with the power of the Beyonders: creator and sole ruler of Battleworld with a rewritten reality.
Earth-616 Main MCU
Designation of the main MCU universe (so named in MoM — comic purists mean the comics world by 616; a glorious nerd fight).
Earth-838 MoM
Illuminati universe: Professor X, Captain Carter, Black Bolt, Mr. Fantastic — wiped out by Wanda.
Earth-828 First Steps
Retro-future world of the Fantastic Four. The number honors Jack Kirby's birthday (8/28).
Earth-10005 Fox universe
World of the old X-Men films. Anchor being: Logan. Part of the MCU multiverse since Deadpool & Wolverine.
Latveria Comics · soon MCU
Small Eastern European state, absolute monarchy under Doom — diplomatically untouchable.
The Void two meanings!
1) The dump at the end of time, guarded by Alioth. 2) Sentry's dark half. Mind the context.
Sentry Thunderbolts*
Bob Reynolds — “the power of a thousand exploding suns”. The most unstable superhuman in the MCU.
Nexus being Loki / WandaVision
Individuals who can massively influence timelines (Wanda, Loki). Rare and dangerous.
He Who Remains Loki S1
Kang variant who won the Multiversal War and created the Sacred Timeline. Killed by Sylvie.
Council of Kangs Quantumania
Assembly of countless Kang variants. Never shown again since 2023 — the biggest open thread.
God of Stories Loki S2
Loki's final form: he holds all timelines together like a living tree.
Franklin Richards First Steps
Son of Reed and Sue. In the comics an omega-level reality shaper — and Battleworld's power source.
Molecule Man not in the MCU yet
The most inconspicuous god of the comics; in Secret Wars 2015 the bomb Doom's power rests on. Keep your eyes open.
Beyonders Comics
Omnipotent beings beyond the multiverse; the trigger of both comic Secret Wars.
Sentinels SDCC trailer
Mutant-hunting giant robots from the X-Men comics. Doom awakens an entire army in the Hall H footage.
Pruning Loki
TVA procedure of “cutting” timeline branches or persons out of time. It all lands in the Void.
Multiversal War backstory
The primordial war between timelines. Doomsday/Secret Wars is, at its core: round two.
Young Avengers assembling
Kamala, Cassie, America Chavez, Kate Bishop, Wiccan — the harvest comes after the saga.
Snap & Blip 2018 / 2023
The Snap erased half of all life, the Blip brought them back five years later. The trauma in between shapes every character of the post-Endgame era.
Variant Loki / TVA
Any alternative version of a person from another timeline — from alligator Loki to the question of whether Doom is one.
Canon event Spider-Verse
Miguel O'Hara's dogma: certain tragedies MUST happen or the universe collapses. Miles disagrees.
Quantum Realm Ant-Man
Subatomic world outside time and space — Endgame's time machine, Kang's prison, Janet's 30-year exile.
Celestials Eternals
Cosmic titans that gestate inside planets. The frozen Tiamut in the Indian Ocean is an adamantium mine today.
Sokovia Accords Civil War
The UN law for hero oversight that split the Avengers. Later quietly buried — the distrust remained.
Symbiotes Venom
Alien lifeforms that amplify hosts (and like eating heads). A remnant has been lying on a counter in Earth-616 since NWH.
Flerken Captain Marvel
Looks like a cat, but is an alien with pocket dimensions in its mouth. Goose scratched out Fury's eye. Not a joke.
