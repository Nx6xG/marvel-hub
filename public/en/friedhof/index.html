What almost happened

The Graveyard

Canceled films, buried shows, lost visions — the Marvel history that never reached theaters. Every grave with what remained of it.

Spider-Man 4 (Raimi)
2007–2010 · † Januar 2010
✝
The Vulture that never flew

After Spider-Man 3, Raimi was set to continue with Maguire: John Malkovich was practically cast as the Vulture, Anne Hathaway as Felicia Hardy, concept art exists. Raimi could not get the script to his standard in time — and walked rather than deliver a film he did not believe in. Sony killed the project two months before shooting.

What remained: The Amazing reboot rose directly from these ashes — and Raimi returned to Marvel in 2022 with Multiverse of Madness. Maguire's NWH line about still trying hits twice as hard.
Edgar Wrights Ant-Man
2006–2014 · † Mai 2014
✝
Eight years of work, six weeks before the shoot

Wright (Shaun of the Dead) developed Ant-Man from 2006 — his 2012 test footage thrilled Comic-Con. But the 2014 MCU wanted connectivity, not an auteur film: Marvel had his script rewritten and Wright left six weeks before shooting. Peyton Reed took over.

What remained: Remnants of Wright's heist structure and Luis' storytelling montages survive in the finished film — he kept a story credit. The most prominent creative rupture in MCU history, still cited in every debate about Marvel's directorial leeway.
Inhumans — der Kinofilm
2014–2016 · † April 2016
✝
From Phase 3 into the series grave

In 2014 Marvel announced Inhumans as a Phase 3 theatrical film — a mutant substitute while Fox held the X-Men. After the power struggle between Kevin Feige and Ike Perlmutter, the film quietly vanished from the slate. The remains were recycled in 2017 as a cheaply produced TV series — 11% on Rotten Tomatoes.

What remained: Black Bolt got a 90-second comeback in Multiverse of Madness in 2022, spectacular exit included. Kamala's series keeps the Inhuman door ajar.
The Sinister Six
2013–2015 · † Februar 2015
✝
The villain franchise Sony wanted

After Amazing Spider-Man 2, Sony planned its own universe: Drew Goddard (Cabin in the Woods) was to direct the Sinister Six as an antihero film, plus a Venom solo and a spider-women film. ASM2's underperformance and the Sony hack buried it all — the historic Marvel deal came instead.

What remained: No Way Home assembled five multiverse villains in 2021 — the closest the Sinister Six ever got to the screen. Goddard did end up at Marvel later: as the creator of Daredevil.
Silver & Black
2017–2018 · † August 2018
✝
Silver Sable & Black Cat

Gina Prince-Bythewood was set to direct the mercenary-and-master-thief duo for Sony's Spider-Verse. After several delays, Sony split the film into two solo projects that never materialized. Prince-Bythewood made The Old Guard and The Woman King instead.

What remained: Black Cat remains the most conspicuous never-filmed Spider character — and a permanent guest in every casting rumor for upcoming Spider-Man films.
Avengers: The Kang Dynasty
2022–2023 · † December 2023 — became Doomsday
✝
The saga endgame that had to go

Announced at Comic-Con 2022 as the crowning of the Multiverse Saga: Destin Daniel Cretton directing, Kang as the Thanos successor, the Council of Kangs already established. After Jonathan Majors' conviction, Marvel parted ways with him — and with the entire saga plan. Cretton moved to Spider-Man: Brand New Day.

What remained: From the ashes rose the biggest casting coup in cinema history: RDJ as Doom, the Russos back — Doomsday. The Council of Kangs still awaits cleanup.
Gambit (Channing Tatum)
2014–2019 · † 2019, Disney takeover
✝
Five years, four directors, zero shooting days

Tatum fought for a Gambit solo from 2014: Rupert Wyatt, Doug Liman and Gore Verbinski took turns attached, the budget stood, shooting dates were announced three times. Then Disney bought Fox — and buried the project for good. Tatum later spoke openly about his frustration.

What remained: Deadpool & Wolverine turned the frustration into canon: Tatum's Gambit debuted in the Void as the one whose film never came — and became so popular he is now in the Doomsday cast. The best happy ending on this list.
X-Force (Drew Goddard)
2017–2019 · † 2019, Disney takeover
✝
Deadpool's team film

After Deadpool 2, Goddard was to direct X-Force with Reynolds, Brolin's Cable and Zazie Beetz's Domino — the R-rated counterpoint to the well-behaved X-Men. The Fox takeover froze everything; Marvel chose Deadpool solo in the MCU.

What remained: The X-Force name lives on as a Deadpool 2 gag (the parachute massacre). Cable and Domino have been waiting to return since 2018.
New Mutants — die Horror-Trilogie
2015–2020 · † with the 2020 release
✝
The horror film that was not allowed to be one

Josh Boone planned a horror trilogy in the X-Men universe — part 2 with Antonio Banderas as Emmanuel da Costa, part 3 featuring Warlock. Fox demanded reshoots for more horror that never happened; Disney inherited the film and dumped it without comment in the pandemic summer of 2020.

What remained: The finished first part remained half a film — and Magik actress Anya Taylor-Joy became a star anyway. Her return as Magik is a perennial rumor for the X-Men reboot.
Blade 4 & das Wesley-Snipes-Ende
2004–2012 · † after Trinity
✝
How a trilogy ended in conflict

After Blade: Trinity (2004) a fourth film was discussed — but the shoot had been wrecked by conflicts between Snipes and director David S. Goyer, including a later lawsuit. Snipes' tax trial then ended any sequel fantasy.

What remained: Snipes got his comeback 20 years later in Deadpool & Wolverine — insisting there has only ever been one Blade — while Marvel's Ali reboot keeps circling the runway.
Agent Carter Staffel 3 & der Netflix-Kahlschlag
2016–2019 · † 2016 / † 2019
✝
Two eras, one ending

Agent Carter was canceled in 2016 after two seasons despite fan campaigns — season 3 was roughly sketched. In 2018/19 Netflix axed Daredevil, Luke Cage, Iron Fist, Jessica Jones and Punisher within months as Disney+ appeared on the horizon: six shows, one clear-cut.

What remained: Hayley Atwell returned as Captain Carter (What If, MoM), and the Netflix corner was partially canonized: Cox, D'Onofrio and Bernthal continue their roles in the MCU today.
Sonys Spider-Man Universe (SSU)
2018–2024 · † December 2024
✝
Six films, zero Spider-Man

Venom unexpectedly earned $856 million in 2018 — Sony built a universe on top of it without its main character: Morbius flopped twice, Madame Web became a meme catastrophe, Kraven arrived as the first R-rated film and left as the last film, period. After Venom 3, Sony pulled the plug.

What remained: The debris is scattered across this wiki: a symbiote on an MCU counter, a teased god (Knull), a baffling Vulture cameo. Sony has since focused on Spider-Man himself and the Spider-Verse.
Fant4stic 2
2015 · † within its release year
✝
The sequel that died in the credits

Fox had already dated the sequel to the 2015 reboot for June 2017 — then the film hit theaters: 9% on Rotten Tomatoes, a public fight between director Josh Trank and the studio, Trank's famous deleted tweet about the fantastic version no one would ever see.

What remained: The third attempt only succeeded in the MCU: First Steps (2025) demonstratively did many things differently — brighter tone, family at the center, Galactus with a body.
Das Serien-Zwischenreich: Ghost Rider, Helstrom & Co.
2019–2020 · † before or after one season
✝
Hulu's failed horror universe

Marvel Television planned a dark 'Adventure into Fear' universe at Hulu: a Ghost Rider series with Gabriel Luna was announced and buried without shooting, Helstrom (2020) remained the only realized show — canceled after one season when Feige took over the TV division.

What remained: Ghost Rider sat orphaned for six years — until the 2026 SDCC bomb: Ryan Gosling takes over the Spirit of Vengeance in Doomsday.
Verlorene Regie-Visionen: Ava DuVernay, Patty Jenkins & Scott Derrickson
2014–2020 · three exits, three reasons
✝
The films that almost existed in another form

Patty Jenkins left Thor 2 over creative differences (she wanted a space Romeo-and-Juliet story), Ava DuVernay declined Black Panther because Marvel offered too little control, Scott Derrickson exited before Doctor Strange 2 because his horror approach went too far.

What remained: Every gap became an opportunity: Coogler turned Black Panther into an Oscar film, Raimi inherited MoM complete with horror signature — and Jenkins made Wonder Woman for the competition.
Justice League Mortal? Nein: Marvels vergessene 90er
1984–1998 · † in development limbo
✝
James Cameron's Spider-Man & Carpenter's Ghost Rider

Before Marvel films worked, giants failed: James Cameron wrote a Spider-Man treatment in the 90s (with organic web-shooters and an Electro/Sandman duo) that drowned in rights litigation. Stan Lee pitched Ghost Rider to John Carpenter, and a Black Panther film with Wesley Snipes was stuck for years.

What remained: Cameron's organic web-shooters survived into Raimi's trilogy. And that era's rights litigation is the reason Sony holds Spider-Man to this day.
